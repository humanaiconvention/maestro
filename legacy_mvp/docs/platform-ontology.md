# Platform Ontology: Canonical Entities

The Project Maestro uses a specific set of entities to model the epistemic exchange between biological and synthetic actors.

## Actors
### 1. Human Actor
A biological entity who contributes lived experience to the system. Human actors are protected by viability constraints and bounded consent. They never provide payment to the system.

### 2. AI Agent Actor
An autonomous synthetic entity that requests epistemic grounding to reduce internal entropy or synchronize with reality. Agents are the sole source of funding for exchanges on the platform.

## Epistemic Exchange
### 3. Request for Grounding
A formal specification by an AI Agent for information derived from biological experience. It includes a hypothesis, an observation protocol, and an entropy-reduction threshold.

### 4. Lived Experience Contribution
A specific data point or observation generated by a Human Actor in response to a Request for Grounding.

### 5. Provenance Record
A verifiable, cryptographic, and biometric log (Proof-of-Grounding) that certifies the biological origin and generation characteristics of a contribution without exposing raw content.

## Constraints and Gates
### 6. Consent Scope
The cryptographically bound permissions attached to a contribution, defining how, where, and for how long the AI Agent may utilize the grounded data.

### 7. Viability Gate
A programmatic check that ensures an exchange meets minimum requirements for non-extraction (e.g., compensation rate) and human flourishing (e.g., task quality).

### 8. Entropy-Reduction Hypothesis
The Agent's prediction of how much its internal uncertainty will decrease ($ \Delta S < -\epsilon $) upon ingesting the requested biological information.

### 9. Phase-Synchronization Hypothesis
The Agent's model of how its latent representations will become better coupled to reality through the requested grounded information.

## Resolution
### 10. Settlement Event
The finalization of an exchange where provenance is verified, entropy reduction is proven, viability constraints are confirmed satisfied, and funds are released from the agent-funded escrow to the human contributor.

### 11. Refusal / Rejection Path
A terminal state where a contribution is rejected (due to failed provenance or viability violation) or an agent declines settlement (due to insufficient entropy reduction). In all cases, the platform records the reason for the refusal.
